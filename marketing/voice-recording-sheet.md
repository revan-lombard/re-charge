# Voice recording sheet

Three short recordings, one per video, about 30 seconds each. Ten minutes in total, including retakes.

## Before you start (2 minutes)

- **Room:** a small room with soft things in it (bedroom, a car parked somewhere quiet, a cupboard full of clothes). Avoid kitchens and bathrooms, which echo.
- **Quiet:** fridge, fan, TV and aircon off. Phone on aeroplane mode, so nothing pings mid-take.
- **Phone:** use the built-in **Voice Recorder** app (Samsung) or **Voice Memos** (iPhone).
  - Hold it about a hand's width from your mouth, slightly below, so your breath doesn't hit it.
  - Keep the same distance the whole time.
- **Voice:** stand up if you can. Smile while you talk: people can hear it, and it's where the enthusiasm comes from.
- **Pace:** a little quicker than normal conversation, as if you're telling a friend something good.

## How to record

- Make **one recording per video** and read its six lines **in order**.
- **Leave a clear 2-second pause between lines.** Count "one-thousand, two-thousand" in your head. The pause is how the lines get cut apart and placed in their scenes.
- Fluffed a line? Stop, and start that video's recording again. Don't try to fix it mid-take.
- Name the recordings **email**, **question** and **invoice**.
- Each line has about the time shown below. The last line can be a bit longer. Don't rush: lines that run slightly over are sped up a touch automatically (up to 12%), and anything more just starts a moment later.

## Tips for sounding natural

- **Lift the questions.** Let your voice go up at the end of "...all day?" and "...by hand?". The opening question is the hook.
- **Land the numbers.** Slow down a fraction on "Fifteen minutes?", then a quick, pleased "Now it's two."
- **Say "AI" as two letters:** "ay-eye".
- **Say it to one person.** Picture a business owner you know, not "an audience".
- **The last line is an invitation**, not an advert. Warm, not loud.
