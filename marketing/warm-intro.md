# Warm intro messages

For people who already know you. Send them one at a time and personally, never as a group message.
Change the first line so it sounds like you and fits the person.

The goal is an **introduction**, not a sale. Asking "do you know anyone?" gets more honest replies than
"do you want to buy?", and people often answer "actually, us."

---

## WhatsApp (short)

Hi [name], hope you're well!

Quick one: I've started a small business on the side helping companies get AI to do their admin, things like answering routine emails, capturing invoices and finding answers in their own documents. A person still checks everything.

Do you know anyone who's drowning in that kind of paperwork? Even one name would really help.

And if it's you, I'm happy to have a free 30-minute look at what's eating your team's time. No obligation.

---

## Email (a bit longer)

**Subject:** A quick favour

Hi [name],

I hope things are going well at [their business / with you].

I've started Re-Charge, a small business helping companies use AI for the repetitive work their teams do by hand: routine customer emails, typing invoices into the accounts, finding answers buried in policy documents, writing up meetings. A person still approves anything that matters.

I'm looking for a few businesses to work with first, and I'd value an introduction to anyone you know whose team is stuck doing that kind of work.

I start with a free 30-minute call to see whether it's worth doing at all. Sometimes the honest answer is "a better spreadsheet would do", and I'll say so.

You can see a few examples here: https://re-charge.co.za/ai-demos

Thanks, and no pressure at all.

[your name]

### Before you send

- Don't contact your employer's clients, suppliers or subcontractors until you've checked your contract.
- Keep a list of who you messaged and when (Pipeline in the panel), and follow up once after 5–7 days if there's no reply. Never more than once.
